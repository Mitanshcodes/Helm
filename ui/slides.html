Helm is designed for gamers with limited hand and arm movement who can still move their head or face, even a little, even on one side.

A webcam in, a real Xbox controller out

  1. Webcamany laptop camera
  2. Face trackinghead pose and 52 expression scores
  3. Your calibrationyour range, your strength
  4. Your gesturesthresholds plus ones Helm learns
  5. Mappingsticks, triggers, buttons
  6. Virtual Xbox padany game, no mods

Everything runs on the laptop. Video never leaves it, and nothing is recorded. An optional straw and pressure sensor add breath as one more input.

The controller adapts to the player

  • Your range is full range. Calibration measures how far you can comfortably turn, so ten degrees of movement can still mean full stick.
  • It learns your gestures. Show Helm any movement five times, then talk and look around so it learns what not to fire on.
  • Analog, not just on and off. A half-open jaw is half throttle.
  • It notices fatigue. When a gesture weakens, its threshold comes down and the screen says so.
  • A coach builds the mapping. An agent reads the game's controls and your reliable gestures, drafts a mapping, checks it, and revises it after a session.

What comes next

  • Test with real users. Reach out to organizations such as AbleGamers and Makers Making Change.
  • Open source, free for players. It runs on a webcam people already own.
  • More places to play. A Linux backend, a mouse-and-keyboard mode, and a shared library of per-game profiles.
  • Beyond games. The same pipeline can drive desktop navigation and communication shortcuts.
